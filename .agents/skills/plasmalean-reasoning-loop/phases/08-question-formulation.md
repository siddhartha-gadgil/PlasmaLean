# Phase 1d (exploratory research): Question formulation

Turn an open-ended prompt into precise, ranked research questions, without pretending they are
already proved. Save to `<task>/02_research_questions.md`.

1. **Read the context:** `<task>/01_problem_formalization.md` and, if present,
   `<task>/02_literature_review.md`, together with the related goals, questions and notes of the
   project graph.
2. **Extract axes of variation:**
   - hypotheses to weaken or strengthen;
   - conclusions to sharpen, quantify or replace;
   - ambient categories, dimensions, regularity classes or finiteness assumptions to vary;
   - examples and counterexamples suggested by the definitions.
3. **Generate five to ten precise questions**, each with a mathematical statement or objective,
   motivation, expected difficulty and first probes to try.
4. **Generate test objects:** for each primary candidate, representative examples and likely
   counterexample families.
5. **Rank** and choose two or three primary questions, balancing plausibility, novelty,
   tractability and relevance to the prompt and to the project's goals.
6. **Guardrails.** Mark speculative claims as questions or conjectures, not theorems.

For structured output, follow `../schemas/08-question-formulation.json`.
