The stellarator contract (30 October 2026)

User-approved restructuring (30 October 2026). This section re-aims the project. It takes precedence over older formulations of the goals in RG.1 and §0. History, verified results and declaration names are unchanged; only the framing and the priorities change.

Rationale. Tokamaks are not stable in the sense that matters for a reactor: they rely on a large inductive or driven toroidal plasma current, which brings current-driven instabilities (kinks, tearing), disruptions and the need for continuous current drive. That is the reason for stellarators. So the plan should not mainly try to prove the existence of non-tokamak or non-axisymmetric solutions (a Grad counterexample, a field that is "not exactly symmetric"), but of solutions with the additional properties a stellarator needs. Those properties exclude tokamaks and are achievable by stellarators, and the decisive ones concern the drifts of thermal ions and electrons.

The contract. A candidate satisfies the stellarator contract when, for one decoded field and equilibrium with common witnesses:

C1–C4 are about the field and its stability; C5 makes the temperature requirement explicit. C2 couples into C5: poor omnigeneity means large 1/ν neoclassical losses (χ ∝ ε_eff^{3/2} in the 1/ν regime), which can make burn inaccessible, as in the proved Stellaris obstructions (stellaris_measured_fren_no_access_steady, stellaris_measured_fren_no_access_loss, Q-13).

How this re-aims the project.

  • Non-axisymmetry and "not exactly symmetric" become consequences of C1 (with C4), not goals: a

    • current-free field with ι > 0 cannot be axisymmetric. Results whose only content is non-axisymmetry (Q-17, the Grad counterexamples of N-28) are mathematical infrastructure, not design progress.

  • C1 and C2 are the new primary filters for candidates. A candidate that carries a net toroidal

    • current of the order of the field-producing current is tokamak-like under C1 however non-axisymmetric it is.

  • C2 must be stated for thermal particles. Alpha confinement (G-6, Q-1) is one part of C2, not a

    • substitute for it.

  • Tokamak models (Solov'ev) stay in the library as models for G-2 and as test beds for techniques;

    • they are not stellarator candidates.

  • The goal registry RG.1 is restated around C1–C5 (G-1, G-2, G-4, G-8 restated; G-10, G-11 new),

    • the questions Q-18–Q-22 are added, and the work selection RG.5 now puts current-free and omnigenous candidates first.

  • Definitions of net toroidal current, bounce-averaged radial drift, omnigeneity and ε_eff, and the

    • C5 thermal clause, are normative specifications in notes/formalization/00-model-contracts.md (contract entries CurrentFreeTransform, BounceAveragedDrift/Omnigenous/ EffectiveRippleBound and ThermalPerformance), not yet in Lean as contract predicates (Q-21).

1. Corrections to the notes (do these first; formalizing them as written would be unsound)

Several statements in the newer notes are false or vacuous. Each correction gets a sanity theorem or a counterexample in Lean.

  1. **Subharmonic means no interior maximum, not no interior minimum. Both PDE §2.2 and microturbulence §4, check 3 say that \|B\|² subharmonic rules out a strict interior local minimum of \|B\|. That is backwards: \|x\|² is subharmonic with a minimum at 0, and minimum-B mirror fields (Ioffe/baseball coils) are vacuum fields. The correct statement is that \|B\| has no strict interior local maximum** in vacuum. The real obstruction to min-B and maximum-J in toroidal vacuum fields is a different theorem; find the source (e.g. Hall–McNamara, Bernardin–Moses–Tataronis) and state it precisely. Lean: vacuum_normSq_no_strict_interior_max, plus a counterexample showing that a vacuum field can have an interior minimum of \|B\|.
  2. The magnetic 2-form vanishes on flux surfaces. B is tangent to Σ_ψ, so ω_B restricted to Σ_ψ is zero. The line bundle on a flux torus is therefore flat (degree 0), and flux_degree > 0 in the holomorphic note is physically wrong. holomorphicSectionDim is also true by definition, so it checks nothing. Further correction (24 October): a flat connection's periods encode fluxes; its holonomy at one surface does not determine the rotational transform. The latter requires an oriented derivative ratio of fluxes across surfaces. Fix cycle orientations before identifying those derivatives. Re-target on periods, the conformal modulus τ(ψ), and the Hodge inequality in §11.6 (compare Challenge6_ConformalTorusModulus).
  3. 2-D ideal MHD global regularity is open. BKM §2.2 claims that helically symmetric MHD is globally regular. The planar 2-D fully viscous/resistive problem has global regularity; a helical reduction needs its own theorem and hypotheses. Quasisymmetry alone is not a PDE symmetry (§11.4). The Riccati law dY/dt ≤ −νY + CεY² is a heuristic, not a theorem. Keep only the conditional statement: if the differential inequality holds, then Y stays bounded below the threshold. Mark the inequality itself as research (R3).
  4. Vacuous kernel contracts. FutakiObstructionCertificate is correct but needs ⟪μ x, u⟫ constant over all x. All the physics is in proving that hypothesis for an MHD ansatz family. CommutedWallClearanceCertificate is an arithmetic inequality with no link to any PDE. Each contract must come with a theorem that produces it from model hypotheses, or it stays out of the library.
  5. Unchecked identities. These need derivation and sympy checks before Lean: the relative-entropy dissipation identity (BKM §3.1), the Helander–Mackenbach Â formula (extract it exactly from MPH 2022/2023), and the Klainerman–Sobolev step H² ↪ L∞ (commuting only with u and b controls tangential derivatives, so an elliptic-regularity step across surfaces is required).

The skills' adversarial-review step (§3, lane E) is how we stop this kind of error from recurring.

Choose a coil geometry, operating parameters, a compatible equilibrium or approximate evolution, and a nontrivial set of initial perturbations. An all-time result is a later target when the same estimates close globally; a finite operating interval with explicit margins is already useful.

The intended chain is:

  1. Certify the external vacuum field and its coil realization.
  2. Construct or validate the finite-pressure state for the total field, with equation of

    3. state, boundary data and pressure matching.

  3. Re-evaluate quasisymmetry and confinement after plasma response.
  4. Bound a full evolution residual and prove existence/continuation for a neighbourhood of

    5. initial data.

  5. Prove perturbation stability, core-temperature and yield bounds as distinct conclusions.

Vacuum quasisymmetry alone does not make step 2 automatic. A pointwise upper bound p_thermal <= p_eq is a pressure-budget screen, not equality in force balance. All-time bounds for supplied solutions do not establish their global existence. Positive measure in velocity space should be accompanied by a physically meaningful energy and pitch range, rather than used as a replacement for a robustness margin.

11.3 The concrete first theorem to implement

Use the flat periodic benchmark exactly as in Pizzocchero–Tassi, Proposition 4.3: divergence-free, zero-mean fields on (R/2pi Z)^3, smooth initial data and approximation, Sobolev order s>5/2, and the stated viscosity/resistivity coefficients.

Let epsilon_s bound the full PDE residual, delta_s the initial discrepancy, and D_s,D_(s+1) the approximation norms. First prove a checked nonnegative control function satisfies

$$R'\ge-\mu R+ (\widehat G_sD_s+\widehat K_sD_{s+1})R+ \widehat G_sR^2+\epsilon_s,\qquad R(0)\ge\delta_s,$$

where mu=min(nu,eta) in this normalization. Then prove the PDE bridge yields existence and ||U-U_a||_{H^s}<=R before the control horizon. Certify a horizon strictly greater than T_burn to cover the requested closed interval without an endpoint gap.

The abstract scalar lemma is an early deliverable. The full source theorem additionally needs local well-posedness, continuation, bilinear estimates and norm conventions. Keep those gaps visible. With a smooth finite Fourier approximation, the full nonlinear residual has explicitly computable modes outside the truncation: no hypothetical bound on an unknown true solution's Fourier tail should be substituted for the residual theorem.

For the bounded-domain route use Kozono–Shimizu–Yanagisawa (2025). Their small harmonic background and small perturbation conditions are not a reactor-scale certificate. First derive explicit geometry-dependent constants or a validated estimate for a particular domain. The background flux lives in a harmonic subspace, so the magnetic Poincaré inequality must be stated on its complement.

11.4 Tests for a symmetric or approximately symmetric plasma ansatz

Before numerical optimization or Lean implementation, write the ansatz in the full equations and calculate the residual.

An exact positive fixture is the standard Alfvénic class on the flat three-torus: a(x,t)=a_0(x+t b_0), v=a, b=b_0+a, with constant b_0, smooth periodic divergence-free a_0, and constant total pressure. Direct substitution gives a global smooth ideal solution and BKM integral 2T ||curl a_0||_infty. For nonzero b_0 and mean-zero a_0 this lies outside the zero-mean magnetic-field hypothesis of the Pizzocchero–Tassi benchmark. Formalize it separately, with the absence of toroidal-vessel confinement, thermodynamic closure, and transverse perturbation stability explicit. It tests the ansatz mechanism without claiming a stellarator construction.

  • A genuine Killing generator preserving all coefficients and boundary conditions is a useful

    • benchmark. Symmetry of initial data is preserved only after the equation and uniqueness hypotheses have been supplied.

  • Quasisymmetry of magnetic strength is generally a guiding-centre property. It does not make

    • the pressure gradient, Laplacian or force operator commute with its generator.

  • The local example B=e_z, X=(1+y)e_x, p=y has zero scalar QS error but

    • [X,grad p]=-e_x. Include it as a negative sanity target.

  • Approximate invariance needs norms of L_X g, coefficient/background derivatives, and boundary

    • mismatch or, preferably, a complete reconstructed residual. Do not posit a Riccati estimate whose nonlinear coefficient is only the scalar QS error.

  • Exact 2-D, helical and axisymmetric ansätze are different reductions. A theorem inside one

    • invariant class does not prove stability against perturbations outside that class.

  • Field-line KAM confinement is an ODE statement for a static field. It does not control

    • MHD current-sheet formation, bulk instability, kinetic turbulence or a moving interface.

The earlier elliptic-profile idea remains an optional field-construction test: study the real periodic branch of y^2=[psi-(x-1)^2](2-x), then solve or obstruct the separate embedded Maxwell/coordinate compatibility problem. Do not assume that an elliptic curve or a prescribed strength function already supplies a realizable three-dimensional field. Recheck any earlier claimed obstruction with its exact axis, coefficients and hypotheses.

11.5 Perelman-inspired work: a testable functional programme

The target is a physically derived estimate with useful coercivity. Start with existing energy/relative-entropy identities rather than assuming that a Ricci-flow formula transfers.

For any candidate functional:

  1. Specify the evolution, unknowns, domain, boundaries, forcing and reference state.
  2. Differentiate it exactly, retaining reference-profile, boundary, reaction and compression

    3. terms. Record all signs and regularity assumptions.

  3. Test it against explicit solutions and thermal-runaway examples.
  4. Prove which norm or operating quantity it controls, including constants.
  5. Check scaling and whether concentration can leave the functional bounded.
  6. Only then test whether its dissipation controls a continuation criterion.

Two immediate outputs are the corrected thermal entropy identity in notes/theory/relative-entropy-dissipation.md and a core-yield lower bound from verified temperature/fuel/reactivity bands. Total heat alone does not prevent a cold core. A generic entropy inequality does not bound current or vorticity suprema. Nonnegative ideal-MHD energy does not imply coercivity in all displacement directions: neutral/relabeling modes require a specified quotient or constraint.

Use Tao's 2007 discussion and Perelman's original paper for the methodological question, not as a missing MHD theorem. The counter-propagating Alfvén-wave structure has already supported a GR-inspired stability theorem in He–Xu–Yu; its spatial-separation estimates on Euclidean space need replacement in a bounded toroidal geometry.

11.6 Concrete entry points for topology, differential geometry and complex geometry

Topology. Prove the following bridge from the pointwise kernel. If sufficiently smooth divergence-free fields B,J satisfy J×B=grad p, then on each compact connected regular pressure-level component without boundary they give a tangent frame. Curling force balance gives [B,J]=0. The component is a torus. State compactness, regularity and connectedness componentwise; this says nothing about critical levels or the magnetic axis. A second target is flux/harmonic-sector bookkeeping for E4 and E7.

Differential geometry. Prove the gradient commutator, a genuine symmetry-preservation theorem, and a dictionary between Euclidean fields and supplied flux coordinates. Extend current coarea bridges to a clearly specified family. For near-axis QS, distinguish formal compatibility from convergence and embedded global realization. Use negative examples as acceptance tests.

Complex analysis and geometry. The most direct quantitative target is already identified: actual-coil KAM bounds in notes/theory/coil-kam-status.md. Develop anisotropic holomorphic strips, a torus-adapted complex neighbourhood, cancellation-aware bounds for groups of Biot–Savart segments, and certified lower bounds on the toroidal component. Compare the new residual bound with the exact current KAM threshold; report a failed bound as a failed certificate, not as nonexistence of an invariant torus. Existing modules BiotSavartComplex, StripPaleyWiener, CauchyTwoVar, FourierSumHolo and AnalyticComposition provide starting points.

For a smaller Hodge/complex-torus task, take Im tau>0 and a smooth real closed one-form on C/(Z+tau Z), with periods a,b along the canonical cycles 1,tau. For the flat metric, or any metric in its conformal class, prove:

$$\int |\alpha|^2\,dA \ge \frac{|b-a\tau|^2}{\operatorname{Im}\tau},$$

with equality if and only if the form is its harmonic representative. Identify physical periods only after fixing orientations. The current flux-holonomy note needs a sign audit: if sigma=h dtheta wedge dphi, then i_B sigma=h(Btheta dphi-Bphi dtheta). A point of the Jacobian alone does not recover the rotational transform. A magnetic two-form restricted to a flux surface vanishes, so it does not provide a positive-degree line bundle there.

Optional new benchmark. Audit the 21 October 2026 López preprint on an exact nonsymmetric vacuum field from a Weierstrass/algebraic construction. Its proposed nested tori and closed lines with constant rational transform are interesting formalization targets. This is a newly posted construction to check, not an established QS, coil-realization, stability or robust-confinement result; its computer-assisted root/embedding conditions need independent review.
