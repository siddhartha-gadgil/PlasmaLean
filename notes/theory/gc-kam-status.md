## 1. What is proved

### 1.1 The KAM theorem for fields with an inverse-square-root node **[Lean]**

The existing interface `FlowField` (`Design/KamFlowField`) takes polynomial field components.
`B̃` is polynomial in `(u, z, c, s)` and one algebraic variable `W = q^{−1/2}`, where
`q = R⁸|B|²`. The new interface takes expressions with the node `isqrt c d = (c + d)^{−1/2}`
(`PolyExprS`, `Theory/KAM/PolyExprS`).

- `Theory/KAM/PolyExprSStrip`: strip validity `SValid`, plus three rules:
  - chain rule `PolyExprS.dS_evalS` (for the node, `dS w = −½ w³ dS d`, derived from `w²(c + d) = 1`);
  - reality `IsReal.evalS`, `IsReal.derivS`;
  - the partial-derivative sum `deriv_eq_sumS`.
- `Design/KamFlowFieldS` (`FlowFieldS`), with three changes from `FlowField`:
  - the identities are required only at strip-valid tori (`Good`);
  - the flux primitive has a `dφ`-component `P_c`. This is needed because `α̃ = α − ρ b♭`
    contains `R b_φ dφ`, which no polynomial gauge removes;
  - the torsion numerator is defined by its formula.

  `FlowField.toS` embeds every polynomial field (`toS_resR`, `toS_Pof`), so `field14` is an
  instance.
- `Design/KamFlowFrameS`, `KamFlowStepS`, `KamFlowIterationS`: the frame, the Newton step and the
  iteration, with the same constants `SC`.
  - `FlowFieldS.StepH.kam_step` and `FlowFieldS.KamH.kam_flow_torus` are the exact torus theorems.
  - New hypotheses:
    - `GlobalH.hvR/hvZ`: the residual expressions are valid at `avec + Tq·mvec`;
    - `KamH.hvR'/hvZ'`: they are valid at `avec + 2μ(1,1,0,0,1,1)`;
    - `FrameHyp.good`.
  - The flux identity `eta2_flux` gains the term `∂_θ P_c(K)`.

### 1.2 The guiding-centre field **[Lean]**

- `Design/RationalStellaratorGC`:
  - `gcField ρ = modField ρ (field (1/4))` (the `modField` of `Theory/Kinetic/GuidingCentreLittlejohn`);
  - **`gcField_eq`**: off the axis and where `B ≠ 0`,
    `gcField ρ x = B x + (ρ/2 · Q x^{−3/2}) • (B x × ∇Q x)`, with `Q = |B|²` (`Qf`);
  - helper lemmas: `curl_innerSL_smulRight` (`curl(⟨g,·⟩v) = g × v`) and `curl_fieldDeriv`
    (the vacuum Jacobian is curl free).
- `Design/RationalStellaratorGCPoly`, generated by `scripts/gc_kam/gen_gcflow.py`:
  - polynomials: `∂_u, ∂_z, ∂_φ` of `D, n_R, n_z`, the Taylor polynomial `P(u)` of `R^{−3}` (degree 6),
    and the expanded deviation `E = qP² − 1` (275 monomials, over `1/256`);
  - identities, each checked by `ring` pointwise: `curl1..3` (`∇×B = 0`), the symmetry of second
    partial derivatives, and `E_val`, `E_d0`, `E_d1`, `E_dph`;
  - sanity: `EpP_axis` (`E = 0` on the axis).
- `Design/RationalStellaratorGCFlow`, generated. The flow form of `B̃` uses
  `W = P(u)(1 + E)^{−1/2}`, so `W²q = 1` exactly. With `κ` the brackets of the correction:
  - `D̃ = D + (ρ/2) W³ κ_D`, `κ_D = R²(R n_z ∂_uq − 8 n_z q − R n_R ∂_zq)`;
  - `ñ_R = n_R + (ρ/2) W³ κ_R`, `κ_R = R⁵D ∂_zq − R³ n_z ∂_φq`;
  - `ñ_z = n_z + (ρ/2) W³ κ_z`, `κ_z = R³ n_R ∂_φq − R⁵D ∂_uq + 8R⁴Dq`;
  - `P̃_a = P_a − ρRW n_R`, `P̃_b = P_b − ρR²W n_z`, `P̃_c = −ρR²W D`.

  These formulas were derived with sympy (`gc_symbolic.py`) and checked against direct vector
  calculus. The theorems:
  - `gc_divFree` is split into `gc_divK` (`div κ = 12R³(R²D ∂_zq − n_z ∂_φq)`, uses `∇×B = 0`),
    `gc_gradK` (`κ·∇q = 8R²q(...)`), `gc_W3_d*` (`D(W³) = −(3/2)W⁵Dq`) and `gc_Wq` (`W²q = 1`);
  - `gc_flux_a`, `gc_flux_b`, `gc_flux_c`.

  Every proof is a generated `linear_combination` after rewriting with the relations. A monolithic
  certificate for `gc_divFree` needed 7.9 GB, so it was split as above.
- `Design/RationalStellaratorGCInstance`:
  - **`gcFlow ρ : FlowFieldS`**, the guiding-centre field as a KAM field;
  - `gc_node_sq`: validity gives `W²(1 + E) = 1`;
  - **`gcFlow_valid`**: all validity hypotheses reduce to the single inequality
    `PolyExpr.maj a EpP < 1`;
  - **`gc_kam_torus`**: `(gcFlow ρ).KamH c ι u₀ z₀ e₀ μ →` there is an exact real analytic invariant
    torus that is `μ`-close to `(u₀, z₀)`;
  - sanity: `gcFlow_resR_zero` (`ρ = 0` gives `field (1/4)`).

### 1.3 Certificate tool **[Lean]**

`Theory/KAM/InvSqrtApprox`, **`invSqrt_approx_bound`**: an a posteriori bound for an approximate
inverse square root in `𝔸`. Assume `‖x − 1‖_ρ ≤ m < 1`, `V₀V₀⁻¹ = 1`, `‖V₀⁻² − 1‖_ρ ≤ m`,
`‖V₀ − 1‖_ρ ≤ a` with `½(1 − m)^{−3/2} m + a < 2`, and `‖V₀⁻¹‖_ρ ≤ b`. Then
`‖x^{−1/2} − V₀‖_ρ ≤ ½(1 − m)^{−3/2} b² ‖V₀²x − 1‖_ρ`.

The proof identifies `V₀` with the node value `(V₀⁻²)^{−1/2}`; the two square roots are told apart
by invertibility of `w + V₀`. This is the tool for step 1 of §3.

Readings:

- **`ρ = +1/256` is feasible with a margin of about 1500.** The predicted smallness is
  `32κe₀/δ₀⁴ ≈ 6.6e-4` for `e₀ = 3.5e-23`; it is still `0.011` for `e₀ = 6e-22`.
- The field bounds must be taken **sharp at `K₀`** (computed norm plus Lipschitz majorant times

  `maj ∂_u ñ_z = 93.6`) because `maj W³ = maj(P)³ g³` with `g = (1 − maj E)^{−1/2}`. Only `C₂`
  must be a majorant, and that is affordable.
- **`ρ = −1/256` fails node validity** with this expansion: the torus is larger, at minor radius up
  to 0.26 about `R ≈ 1.056`. Possible fixes:
  - an interface variant with `R = R₀ + u` (centre the expansion at the shifted axis; `xB` and
    `Ri` would change);

### 5.1 Method: ball arithmetic in the strip algebra

The residual of the GC torus is not polynomial in the Fourier data (node), and its exact
Kronecker image would have degree ≈ 40 (≈ 300 MB integers). Instead every quantity is enclosed in
a **ball** `‖X − x‖_r ≤ E` with a trigonometric-polynomial centre `x` (integer grid over `2^S`)
that is truncated and rounded after every step; each step is one kernel check.

- `Certificates/KronBall`: `phiK : ℤ[u][v] →+* 𝔸`, centres `ctr S N M w P` of weight
  `w = (d, e, p)`, the phase bridge `evH_phaseF`, the Lipschitz part `Bd_eval_sub(_below)`, the
  truncation identity `node_eq`, steps `node_step`, `norm_step`, `Bd_ctr_kron`.
- `Certificates/KronBallGrid`: packed half grids (`halfGridR`, one literal per row),
  `gridPolyNM`, static `ℓ¹` bounds, Kronecker images, reality (`isReal_ctr_sym`), derivatives
  (`dS_ctr_grid`), embedding into a larger layout (`ctr_embedGrid`), and the complete kernel steps
  `node_full`, `norm_full`, `center_full` (`nodeK`, `normK`, `centerK`).
- `Theory/KAM/PolyExprSMajQ`: kernel-computable majorants of `PolyExprS` trees (`PQS`,
  `maj_le`, `majD_le`, `majD2_le`, `nodesOK_of_all`) — used for node validity and `C₂`.

Two pipelines (oracle `scripts/gc_kam/gc_cert.py`, `ballcert.py`, `slp.py`; emitter
`emit_lean.py`; constants `gc_maj.py`):

- **high resolution** (`N = 40, M = 30`, scale `2^{-101}`, 70 variables, ≈ 60 nodes) at `K₀`:
  the residual (`e₀`) and the distances from `K₀` to the low-resolution torus;
- **low resolution** (`N = 16, M = 12`, scale `2^{-60}`, 114 variables, ≈ 110 nodes) at **every**
  torus within `μ + dist` of the low-resolution torus `K_S`, on every strip `e^r ≤ 74315/2^16`:
  `D̃, ñ` and their `u, z`-derivatives (second-derivative leaves `Design/RationalStellaratorGCPoly2`),
  `P̃`, `W₀`, the inverse-square-root node, and the torsion pieces. No Lipschitz transport is needed:
  the input balls already contain all `μ`-close tori.

The node: `V₀ ≈ (1+E)^{−1/2}` (Newton, oracle); norm nodes `‖V₀−1‖`, `‖V₀²−1‖`, `‖V₀²(1+E)−1‖`
and `invSqrt_approx_bound` give the ball of `(1+E)^{−1/2}` (`vstep`,
`Design/RationalStellaratorGCTorusCommon`); high resolution `ε_V ≤ 4.7 × 10⁻²⁴`.
