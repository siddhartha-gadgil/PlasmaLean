## 1. The target and the model boundary

The useful target is a specified operating window, or an explicitly quantified small-data
global theorem, for a **single** design and plasma model. Distinguish:

1. existence and uniqueness of a plasma evolution;
2. continuation without a finite-time loss of regularity;
3. stability under a stated class and size of perturbations;
4. magnetic-surface and particle confinement;
5. temperature, fuel, deposited heating, and fusion-yield bounds.

These conclusions do not imply one another. A globally smooth solution can lose confinement
or move far from its initial equilibrium. A stationary solution exists forever if its equations
are satisfied; the useful stability statement covers a neighbourhood of initial data.
A statement about field lines or Littlejohn guiding centres is not a theorem about the
time-dependent MHD plasma.

A vacuum coil calculation is a legitimate first design stage. For the finite-pressure stage use
the total field `B = B_ext + B_plasma`. If this total field is curl free, static isotropic
force balance `J × B = grad p` forces `grad p = 0`. Thus a nonconstant pressure profile
requires plasma response or a different force model. Recheck quasisymmetry on the total field.

Every theorem extraction must record domain, topology, boundary conditions, ideal versus
dissipative equations, density/temperature assumptions, units, regularity, forcing, and
solution concept. A flat periodic three-torus is not a solid toroidal vessel in Euclidean space.

## 2. Continuation criteria and the symmetry proposal

### 2.1 The known MHD BKM theorem

For constant-density, ideal incompressible MHD in Alfvén units,

$$
\partial_t v+v\cdot\nabla v-b\cdot\nabla b+\nabla\Pi=0,\qquad
\partial_t b+v\cdot\nabla b-b\cdot\nabla v=0,\qquad
\operatorname{div}v=\operatorname{div}b=0,
$$

Caflisch–Klapper–Steele [1, Theorem 5.1] prove the BKM analogue in their periodic setting
for sufficiently regular data (the original theorem uses Sobolev order at least 3).
A finite maximal smooth lifespan must have

$$
\int_0^{T_*}\left(\|\operatorname{curl}v(t)\|_\infty+
                         \|\operatorname{curl}b(t)\|_\infty\right)\,dt=\infty.
$$

Consequently, finiteness of this integral prevents that loss of regularity. Here
`b = B/sqrt(mu0 rho)`; physical current is `J = curl B/mu0`.
To prove global continuation one needs finiteness on every finite interval, not integrability
over the entire positive half-line. The theorem does not bound this integral from arbitrary
initial data.

The high-order estimate first uses Lipschitz gradients. For a dimensionless Sobolev energy
`E_s`, the relevant schematic chain is

$$
E_s' \le C_s(\|\nabla v\|_\infty+\|\nabla b\|_\infty)E_s,
\qquad
E_s'\le C[1+K(t)\log(e+E_s)]E_s,
$$

where `K = ||curl v||_infty + ||curl b||_infty`, and harmless lower-order terms
are controlled separately. The second inequality requires a logarithmic div–curl estimate.
It is not the ordinary Grönwall estimate with coefficient `C K`.
For example it gives a bound of the form

$$
\log(e+E_s(t))\le
\log(e+E_s(0))\exp\!\left(Ct+C\int_0^t K(r)\,dr\right),
$$

with constants depending on the precise norm and low-order bounds. An explicit certificate
must derive those constants; this displayed schematic estimate is not yet one.

**Other models need other theorems.** Fully viscous/resistive incompressible MHD has stronger
criteria, including a velocity-vorticity criterion [2]. Compressible heat-conducting MHD has
model-specific strain/temperature criteria [3]; density and divergence bounds alone must not
be advertised as a universal thermal-continuation theorem. For example, the finite-conductivity
problem in [3] imposes zero magnetic boundary data and cannot simply be used for a nonzero
stellarator boundary field. Free plasma boundaries require interface and boundary stability
conditions in addition to bulk norms [4]. In particular, [4] uses a ball-type domain and
Taylor-sign conditions; it is not a solid-torus theorem. Record the exact source hypotheses
before importing any of these results.

### 2.2 Quasisymmetry is not an invariant ansatz for the MHD PDE

Quasisymmetry is a useful symmetry of reduced particle dynamics. It need not be generated by
a Euclidean Killing field [5]. For a generator `X` and scalar `f`,

$$
[X,\operatorname{grad}_g f]
 = \operatorname{grad}_g(Xf)
   -(\mathcal L_X g)^\sharp(\operatorname{grad}_g f).
$$

Here the sharp raises one index of the metric Lie derivative. Thus `Xf = 0` need not make the pressure force invariant. In Cartesian coordinates,

$$
[X\cdot\nabla,\Delta]f
 =-2(\partial_iX_j)\partial_{ij}f-(\Delta X_j)\partial_jf.
$$

A simple local negative example is `B = e_z`, `X = (1+y)e_x` on `0<y<1`.
Both fields are divergence free; they commute; `X(|B|)=0`;
`X × B = grad(-y-y^2/2)`; and `L_X(B^flat)=0`.
Nevertheless `p=y` satisfies `Xp=0` while `[X,grad p]=-e_x`.
This disproves the local operator implication, without purporting to be a toroidal equilibrium.

**A positive ansatz benchmark (paper proof; Lean target).** On the flat periodic three-torus,
fix a nonzero constant `b_0` and any smooth, divergence-free, mean-zero `a_0`. Set

$$
a(x,t)=a_0(x+t b_0),\qquad v=a,\qquad b=b_0+a,\qquad \Pi=\text{constant}.
$$

Then `a_t=(b_0 dot grad)a`, while
`(v dot grad)v-(b dot grad)b=-(b_0 dot grad)a`; the induction equation has
the same cancellation. This directly gives a global smooth ideal incompressible solution.
Translation preserves curl suprema, so its BKM integral is exactly
`2T ||curl a_0||_infty`. In Elsässer variables, `z_-=v-b=-b_0` is constant.
This standard unidirectional Alfvénic class is a useful nontrivial verification fixture.
Its magnetic mean is nonzero, so it is separate from the zero-mean benchmark in section 2.3.
It proves neither confinement in a toroidal vessel, thermal performance, nor stability
against perturbations leaving the ansatz. Global existence here is proved by substitution;
BKM is a consistency check.

Before using a symmetric ansatz, prove invariance of the complete equations under an action
preserving the metric, domain, boundary data, background fields, and constitutive tensors.
A genuine isometric action is a useful benchmark. Even helical spatial symmetry must be
reduced explicitly: geometry and swirl terms cannot be discarded by analogy with planar flow.
Global smoothness of general two-dimensional ideal MHD remains open; fully viscous/resistive
two-dimensional theory is a different problem.

For an approximate ansatz use bounds on the **actual reconstructed PDE residual** and
operator defects, including metric strain `L_X g`, background derivatives, coefficient
defects, and boundary mismatch. There is no established implication

`small scalar QS error => all nonlinear stretching is proportional to that error`.

In particular, the former proposed inequality `Y' <= -nu Y + C epsilon_QS Y^2`
is not an MHD theorem. The scalar comparison implication is valid under appropriate positivity,
initial-value and differential-inequality hypotheses. A theorem producing those hypotheses
from the PDE remains necessary.

### 2.3 The first useful verification target: an a posteriori evolution theorem

Pizzocchero–Tassi [6, Proposition 4.3] provide a more direct bridge to computation.
Start in their exact setting: `T^d = (R/2pi Z)^d`, divergence-free, zero-mean velocity
and magnetic fields, smooth initial data, `nu, eta >= 0`, and `s>d/2+1`.
This is a benchmark, not yet a stellarator-domain theorem.

For a smooth approximation `U_a=(v_a,b_a)`, certify

$$
\begin{aligned}
\|\partial_tU_a-\mathscr A U_a-\mathscr P(U_a,U_a)\|_{H^s}
 &\le \epsilon_s(t),\\
\|U_a(0)-U_0\|_{H^s}&\le\delta_s,\\
\|U_a(t)\|_{H^s}&\le D_s(t),\qquad
\|U_a(t)\|_{H^{s+1}}\le D_{s+1}(t).
\end{aligned}
$$

With `mu=min(nu,eta)` in their normalization (first positive Laplace eigenvalue 1),
a nonnegative continuously differentiable supersolution with

$$
R'\ge-\mu R+
(\widehat G_sD_s+\widehat K_sD_{s+1})R+
\widehat G_sR^2+\epsilon_s,\qquad R(0)\ge\delta_s
$$

certifies the exact solution's existence at least until the control horizon and
`||U-U_a||_{H^s} <= R` before that horizon. The constants are the source's
bilinear-estimate constants and must be justified. A global control function gives global
existence. For a closed requested window `[0,T_burn]`, certify a strictly longer control
interval or prove continuation at its endpoint.

**The residual must be the full continuum residual.** A Galerkin ODE has zero projected
residual while discarded nonlinear modes remain. Include those modes, time-interpolation
error, initial-data discrepancy, arithmetic enclosure error, and uncertainty. Finite samples
of a curl or residual are not a supremum certificate.

Proposed proof layers, all initially marked **open**:

- `Kernel/IncompressibleMHD.lean`: the chosen evolution and solution notion.
- `Theory/Evolution/MHDEnergy.lean`: energy and bilinear estimates.
- `Theory/Evolution/ControlInequality.lean`: scalar comparison, including the error norm's
  upper Dini derivative at zeros.
- `Theory/Evolution/MHDValidation.lean`: the PDE theorem producing the control inequality
  and the continuation conclusion.
- `Certificates/MHDTrajectory.lean`: exact enclosures for a smooth approximate trajectory.
- `Sanity/MHDEvolution.lean`: exact solutions and rejected under-resolved residuals.

Do not replace any producer theorem by a structure that includes its desired conclusion.
Literature-backed hypotheses may be exposed as explicit interfaces while work proceeds, but
the resulting claim must be labelled conditional and not fully formalized.

### 2.4 A bounded-domain route with a genuine topology connection

Kozono–Shimizu–Yanagisawa [7] study the harmonic space

$$
\mathcal H(\Omega)=
\{h:\operatorname{curl}h=0,\ \operatorname{div}h=0,\ h\cdot n=0\}.
$$

For their smooth bounded-domain, normalized viscous/resistive problem, small harmonic
backgrounds in `W^{1,infty}` and small `L^3` perturbations with magnetic perturbation
orthogonal to `H(Omega)` yield global existence and exponential stability.
The boundary conditions are `v=0`, `B.n=0`, `curl B × n=0`.
This is a relevant starting theorem, not a result for arbitrary reactor-strength fields:
its smallness and boundary assumptions are material.

A preparatory identity, for smooth solutions with `b=B-B_*` and harmonic `B_*`, is

$$
\frac{d}{dt}\frac12\int_\Omega(|v|^2+|b|^2)
=-\nu\int_\Omega|\nabla v|^2-\eta\int_\Omega|\operatorname{curl}b|^2.
$$

Prove the boundary cancellations and conservation of the harmonic component. Hodge
coercivity applies on its complement. This is an energy estimate on the smooth lifespan,
not by itself a global strong-existence proof.

The mathematical-relativity analogy also has an existing realization: He–Xu–Yu [8] prove
Alfvén-wave stability near a strong constant field in `R^3`, inspired by
Christodoulou–Klainerman. Oppositely travelling packets separate there. A compact toroidal
device has repeated interactions, so transferring that mechanism requires new estimates.

## 3. Thermal monotonicity, entropy, and useful burn bounds

### 3.1 What to borrow from Perelman

Tao's 18 March 2007 discussion [9] emphasizes a controlled quantity with both useful
coercivity and appropriate scaling. Perelman's entropy [10] supplies special geometric
control for Ricci flow; it does not preclude every Ricci-flow singularity and is not
automatically monotone for MHD on a fixed Euclidean domain.

For MHD, energy and physical entropy already have balance laws under specified boundary
conditions. Total energy or entropy control does not generally control vorticity/current
suprema, and total heat does not guarantee a pointwise hot core. Temperatures need not be
pointwise monotone. Sources, cooling, compression, transport, and fuel depletion matter.

A useful first entropy test uses fixed positive `n(x), T_*(x)`, a symmetric positive
semidefinite tensor `chi(x)` independent of temperature, and

$$
n\partial_tT=\operatorname{div}(n\chi\nabla T)+Q(x,T).
$$

Set `s=T/T_*>0` and

$$
H[T|T_*]=\int_\Omega nT_*(s-1-\log s).
$$

Under enough regularity for the following integrations, the exact identity is

$$
\begin{aligned}
H'={}&-\int_\Omega nT_*\langle\nabla\log s,\chi\nabla\log s\rangle\\
&+\int_\Omega\log s\,\operatorname{div}(n\chi\nabla T_*)
+\int_\Omega(1-1/s)Q(x,T)\\
&+\int_{\partial\Omega}
 [(1-1/s)n\chi\nabla T-\log s\,n\chi\nabla T_*]\cdot n_{\rm out}.
\end{aligned}
$$

The reference-profile and boundary terms cannot be omitted. For constant `T_*`,
zero source and compatible zero-flux or matched Dirichlet data, this gives dissipation.
For a steady, boundary-matched nonconstant `T_*`, the remaining reaction terms have a
favourable sign, for example, if `Q(x,T_*)>=0` and `Q` is non-increasing in temperature.
Alpha heating can violate that sign condition. Temperature-dependent coefficients and an
advected/compressible density require a separate calculation.

The [relative-entropy note](theory/relative-entropy-dissipation.md) contains the corrected
derivation and a counterexample to the old formula. Next formalize the pointwise identity,
its integrated boundary terms, and a precisely delimited sign theorem. A useful Lyapunov
candidate must additionally control the norm required by the chosen stability or continuation
theorem; mere nonnegativity and a name resembling Perelman's functional do not suffice.

### 3.2 Temperature bands and fusion yield

The existing lower/upper comparison barriers are a concrete starting point. They give
a priori bounds for supplied classical solutions. Add local existence and continuation
for the actual radial transport operator, including its degenerate axis and edge conditions,
before claiming global existence.

A conservative yield target avoids an unchecked Jensen argument. If a core of volume at
least `V_core` has `T_min <= T <= T_max`, lower bounds on both fuel densities,
and a verified lower bound `r_min>0` for D–T reactivity on that entire temperature interval,
then

$$
E_{\rm fus}[0,T_{\rm burn}]
\ge Q_{\rm DT}\,n_{D,\min}n_{T,\min}\,r_{\min}V_{\rm core}T_{\rm burn}.
$$

Account for fueling/depletion if densities vary. This is **fusion energy produced**;
net electricity needs deposited-alpha fractions, auxiliary and coil power, conversion
efficiency, and other losses. Bounds on integrated thermal energy alone do not justify this
pointwise core hypothesis.

For coupling, `p_thermal <= p_eq` is only a pressure-budget screening inequality.
It does not imply `J × B = grad p_thermal`. Produce either a steady common
transport/equilibrium state with pressure equality, or a controlled coupled evolution or
nearby-equilibrium branch with an actual force-balance residual.

## 4. CFD and GRMHD methods to adapt

The most useful imports are discrete structural guarantees and continuum error certificates.

| Method | First verifiable property | What it does not establish alone |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Compatible finite elements / constrained transport [11] | Incidence matrices satisfy `D C=0`; an update `B_next=B-dt C E` preserves discrete divergence in exact arithmetic | Helicity, physical boundary conditions, continuum convergence, or all-time smoothness |
| Equilibrium-preserving schemes [12] | The chosen discrete equilibrium has zero update; certify the continuum reconstruction residual separately | Exactness of the physical equilibrium or perturbation stability |
| Entropy-stable resistive MHD [13] | A discrete entropy balance with all nonconservative, diffusion, and boundary terms | Positivity automatically, temperature lower bounds, or continuum BKM control |
| Positivity-preserving GRMHD techniques [14] | Admissibility of density/internal energy under the stated reconstruction, flux, and timestep assumptions | A need to evolve Einstein's equations for a laboratory stellarator |
| A posteriori evolution validation [6] | Full residual and norm enclosures imply a lifespan/error certificate | Reactor applicability without geometry, model, and parameter bridges |

For flux-carrying domains, retain the appropriate harmonic/cohomology sector; do not silently
restrict to the range of a curl with boundary conditions that exclude the physical flux.
The identity `d^2=0` does not by itself conserve magnetic helicity. Helicity also needs
compatible pairings, the evolution law, boundary conditions and time discretization.

Entropy fluxes may contain logarithms or other transcendental functions. Their inequalities
are not automatically decidable by rational arithmetic: use symbolic identities and certified
enclosures with explicit positive-state hypotheses.
