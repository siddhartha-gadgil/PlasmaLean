1. 3D Toroidal Helix vs. Torus Knot & Călugăreanu Theorem

Closing a helix into a torus breaks continuous Euclidean symmetry. The magnetic axis $\gamma_0 : S^1 \to \mathbb{R}^3$ generates on-axis rotational transform $\iota_0$ via its spatial Writhe and cross-section rotation $\delta(s)$:

$$\iota_0 = \mathrm{Wr}(\gamma_0) + \frac{1}{2\pi}\oint \left(\frac{d\delta}{ds} - \tau(s)\right)ds, \qquad \theta(\phi) = \iota(\psi)\phi$$

When $\iota = n/m \in \mathbb{Q}$, closed field lines form $(m, n)$-torus knots (resonant island chains); irrational Diophantine $\iota$ yields dense KAM flux surfaces.

2. Double Fourier Boundary & Near-Axis Parameters

Each flux surface $\Sigma_s \cong T^2$ is parameterized by Fourier modes $(R_{m,n}, Z_{m,n})$ with $N_{\text{fp}}$-fold dihedral stellarator symmetry:

$$R(\theta,\phi) = R_{0,0} + \Delta R_{\text{axis}}(\phi) + a\big(1 + \bar{\eta}\cos(\theta - N_{\text{fp}}\phi) + R_{2,1}\cos(2\theta - N_{\text{fp}}\phi)\big)\cos\theta$$

In this toy model, positive bean/triangularity $R_{2,1} > 0$ indents the cross-section on the inboard side and is drawn as deepening a magnetic well ($\partial_\psi B > 0$) while keeping $\det(D\Phi) > 0$ for moderate amplitudes. This is schematic: whether shaping produces a well, and whether the map stays univalent, depends on the full configuration and is not computed here.

3. Bad Curvature, Maximum-$J$ & Microturbulence ($\widehat{\mathcal{A}}$)

Bad Conditions (Red on 3D Torus): Where normal curvature $\kappa_n < 0$ ($\nabla B$ parallel to $\nabla p$) overlaps trapped-particle wells ($|B|$ minima) and steep $\nabla T_i$ ($\eta_i = L_n/L_{T_i} > \eta_{\text{crit}}$), ITG & TEM streamers erupt:

$$\frac{\partial \mathcal{J}_\parallel}{\partial \psi} = -\frac{mv\lambda}{2}\int_{l_-}^{l_+} \frac{\partial_\psi B \, dl}{\sqrt{1-\lambda B}} < 0 \implies \widehat{\mathcal{A}} = \iint \Theta\!\big(\hat{\omega}_*(\hat{\omega}_* - \hat{\omega}_\alpha)\big)\,d\lambda\,dz \to \text{min}$$
