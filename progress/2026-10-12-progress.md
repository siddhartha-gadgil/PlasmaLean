# Verified foundations — 12 October 2026

## Result and limits

The repository now contains a compiled domain library rather than only starter code. It has explicit model laws, analytic sanity witnesses, reusable inequalities, exact rational certificate checks, continuous clearance and field bounds, two end-to-end synthetic examples, and an exact two-loop vacuum-field fixture. The original illustrated landing page and its graphics are retained; its handbook links now include this progress record.

This is **P0/P1 and partial P2 work**, not a certified practical stellarator. The user selected a small vacuum-field demonstrator as the first device-specific direction. The exact two-loop fixture below advances that direction but has not passed the full P2 gate. No claim is made about a real device's confinement, stability, transport, lifetime, or buildability. Successful conditional proofs do not establish applicability of the supplied physical models.

## Quantitative fixtures

### Small vacuum-field demonstrator

This is a deliberately simple **mathematical fixture** for the user-selected vacuum-first direction. It is an axisymmetric coil pair, not yet a stellarator confinement candidate or a construction prescription.

| Item | Exact specification or compiled result |
| --- | --- |
| Centerlines | c₊(t)=(cos(t)/10,sin(t)/10,1/5), c₋(t)=(cos(t)/10,sin(t)/10,−1/5), in metres |
| Parameter and orientation | One turn t∈[0,2π]; counterclockwise viewed from positive z; actual derivative norm 1/10 m/radian |
| Observation region | Every x with Euclidean norm at most 1/20 m; the origin is a proved member |
| Closure and simplicity | Both curves are 2π-periodic and injective on [0,2π); shared endpoints are not treated as a self-intersection |
| Coil-to-coil clearance | At least 2/5 m for every pair of angular parameters |
| Source clearance | At least 3/20 m for every coil angle and every observation point |
| Centerline displacement budget | Perturbations bounded by ε₁ and ε₂ retain clearance at least 2/5−ε₁−ε₂ m; no automatic field or solid transfer |
| Field model | Sum of the two continuous Biot–Savart filament integrals; integrability is proved for this geometry |
| Whole-region amplitude | Norm of B at most (20/9)(abs(μ₀I)+abs(μ₀J)), for all real currents I,J |
| Whole-region spatial variation | For every x,y in the ball, norm of B(x)−B(y) at most (1600/27)(abs(μ₀I)+abs(μ₀J)) times norm of x−y |
| Midpoint, equal currents | B(0)=(0,0,μ₀I/(100 sqrt(1/20)³)); its axial component is strictly positive when μ₀>0 and I>0 |

There is no numerical quadrature or sample-grid assumption in these declarations. The amplitude estimate is an upper bound; it is not a region-wide lower field bound, flux-surface theorem or particle-confinement theorem. The spatial variation theorem is a Lipschitz estimate, not a claim that the field's Fréchet derivative or Maxwell identities have been formalized. Numerical geometry is exact but invented, with no material/provenance claim. Currents and permeability remain symbolic; no conductor gauge, safe current, support load, power supply, thermal operating limit or physical hardware tolerance has been qualified.

The general loop sanity theorem gives Bz(z)=μ₀IR²/(2 sqrt(R²+(z−z₀)²)³) and zero transverse components on the symmetry axis for every positive radius R. It is derived by continuous integration rather than assumed as a law.

### Thermal boundary-value problem

The physical-law bundle is `Engineering.SteadySlab`. With the cooled face at x=0 and hot face at x=h, it specifies T'=dT, dT'=-Q/k, k dT(h)=q, and k dT(0)=hc(T(0)-Tc), with h≥0, k>0 and hc>0. The derivative assumptions hold at all points of [0,h].

The **synthetic** uncertainty box from note 07 is:

| Input | Entire certified range | Units |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Coolant temperature Tc | [300,330] | K |
| Surface heat flux q | [8,10] × 10⁶ | W/m² |
| Volumetric heating Q | [0,20] × 10⁶ | W/m³ |
| Thickness h | [3,4] × 10⁻³ | m |
| Conductivity k | [90,110] | W/(m K) |
| Heat transfer hc | [50000,80000] | W/(m² K) |

Every classical solution for every real input in this box and every x∈[0,h] has T(x)≤44002/45 K, approximately 977.822 K. The margin below the **synthetic** 1000 K limit is at least 998/45 K, approximately 22.178 K. All six parameters are quantified together; this is not a grid test. The corner Tc=330, q=10⁷, Q=2×10⁷, h=1/250, k=90, hc=50000 at x=h attains the bound.

Existence and uniqueness are mathematical statements inside the stated 1-D constant-coefficient model. They do not certify material data, boiling margins, transient loads, 3-D cooling, neutron damage, or manufacturability.

### Nonlinear scalar root

For T(x)=x+(2-x²)/3, center 3/2 and radius 1/2, the exact residual is 1/12 and the derivative is bounded in magnitude by 1/3 on the **entire** ball [1,2]. The invariant-ball budget is 1/12+(1/3)(1/2)=1/4≤1/2. The proof obtains a unique root in that ball without choosing a pre-existing square-root value. This is a validation-pipeline fixture, not a plasma equilibrium.

### Two-block form and rejection tests

The rational tuple (a,b,g,c)=(5,2,2,1) certifies q(x,y)=5x²−4xy+2y²≥x²+y² for all real x,y. Changing the coupling to 3 fails the checker; requesting zero positive margin also fails. The exact omitted-direction example x²−y² shows why positivity on y=0 says nothing sufficient about the whole space.

Intervals with reversed endpoints are rejected. A collapsed interval denotes exactly one rational real value. Decimal mantissa 125 with scale 3 decodes to exactly 1/8; this is not a theorem about a file/text parser.

The zero approximate inverse fixes every x even when F(x)=1 has no root. Separate witnesses satisfying true and false Boolean predicates cannot be composed. Both failures are exact Lean counterexamples, not numerical observations.

## Assumption ledger

| Interface | Supplied assumptions | Supported consequence and exclusions |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `ForceBalance` | Three real coordinate vectors, exact J×B=g | Algebraic tangency; g is not automatically an actual pressure gradient |
| `Equilibrium.StaticSlab` | Positive permeability, differentiable z-dependent fields/pressure, actual curl/divergence laws | Consistent Cartesian MHD examples; no closed torus or confinement claim |
| Abstract energy | One real normed space, continuous time-independent symmetric forms, a differentiable solution through the specified horizon; positive constants when used | Conditional conservation and the named norm bounds; no existence, continuation, nonlinear or all-mode MHD theorem |
| Two-block certificate | Analytic block bounds and same-gauge perturbation bounds must be supplied downstream | Sound scalar margin only; finite positivity is not continuum stability |
| Clearance | Common metric/units, full-domain cover, valid Lipschitz constants and all sample-pair inequalities | Continuous pairwise clearance; sampled data alone cannot instantiate the theorem |
| Filament field | Free-space filament law, consistent units/current orientation; integrability, tangent bound and positive source separation (all discharged for the exact two-loop fixture) | Whole-region amplitude/variation and exact axis formula; no conductor self-field/self-force, formal field differentiability/Maxwell proof, or confinement |
| Contraction | Complete metric space, full closed-ball Lipschitz bound K<1, residual/budget, positive radius where radii-polynomial lemma used | Local fixed point; a physical PDE still needs its own operator, boundaries, gauge and tails |
| Approximate invariant | Differentiable observable along a supplied time interval, uniform derivative bound and loss gap | Finite-time scalar threshold exclusion; no actual particle orbit bundle |
| Thermal slab | Constant coefficients, uniform volumetric load, classical 1-D laws and two boundary fluxes; positive conductivity/cooling | Unique temperature and whole-box synthetic margin; model/data validity is external |
| Robust design | Explicit predicates indexed by the same design, uncertainty state and equilibrium witness | ∀θ∈U, ∃e satisfying all predicates; not one e for every θ, and no physics from merely constructing a record |

The coordinate-vector `Vec3` is used only for algebra/curl. The filament module deliberately uses `EuclideanSpace ℝ (Fin 3)` so its norm is not accidentally the max norm on a function space. ## Remaining integration boundary

The theorem-building loops made progress, but they did **not** resolve the practical-design objective. Repeating a scalar checker cannot establish the missing physical hypotheses. The user has now selected the small vacuum-field demonstrator, and its first exact centerline fixture is above. A practical extension still requires an operating envelope and sourced conductor/load data. A vacuum demonstrator and a finite-beta reactor have substantially different proof obligations; the design skill requires this distinction.

The next mathematical target is full-observation-region field differentiability with an operator enclosure, together with the source-free divergence/curl identities (D1). The generic integral bound, source exclusion, loop regularity and axis identity now compile. The final loop also proved the spatial inverse-cube derivative and its norm bound, and transferred this to full-region field variation. It did not prove differentiation of the parameter-dependent integral. Mathlib's `Analysis/Calculus/ParametricIntegral.lean` supplies dominated differentiation interfaces; applying them still requires the cross-product derivative composition, uniform domination on a source-separated neighborhood, and derivative measurability/integrability. These integration obligations have not yet been discharged in this library. Neither an exact axis formula nor a Lipschitz estimate is a replacement for a Maxwell proof.

After that come swept-solid geometry and finite-conductor error (D3/D4). These targets advance P2; they do not supply P4 or P5. No stagnation or impossibility theorem is claimed merely because those tasks remain unfinished.

For a practical finite-pressure design the unsatisfied dependencies include:

- A genuine toroidal equilibrium with the chosen profiles, boundary/current constraints and an existence certificate (E5–E8).
- The MHD energy/operator bridge on the correct perturbation space, boundary terms, quotient treatment and all-mode tail/coupling estimates (S2/S5/S7/S8). The algebraic block certificate cannot generate them.
- Actual reduced/full-orbit, kinetic/transport/exhaust bounds and model-transfer errors (K1–K10).
- Finite winding packs, sourced critical surfaces, mechanical/thermal/quench, shielding, irradiation/lifetime and access constraints (D4, M3–M10).
- One decoded candidate, correlated uncertainties and coupled feedback closure shared across all those proofs (N9/D8).

The library intentionally leaves these obligations open rather than introducing assumed global theorems. No research impossibility or instability is inferred from their absence.
