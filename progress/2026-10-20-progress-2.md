# Phase 1 (Cycle 1): burn temperature, transport comparison, vacuum maximum principle — 20 October 2026

## Outcome

This is the first implementation cycle of the [implementation plan](implementation-plan.md). It adds **13 Lean modules**: three new kernel files, three sanity files, and theory, certificate and design modules. The library has no project axioms, and the audit passes for 2383 declarations. The headline results address goal **G2** (keeping the plasma hot long enough), which had no Lean content before this run:

- **0-D burning plasma (model rung M1).** For any supplied trajectory obeying the D-T power balance law, the temperature stays at or above 10 keV for **all t ≥ 0**. Parameters: reactor scale n = 10²⁰ m⁻³, V = 830 m³, carbon impurities with Z_eff = 1.7 (so fuel fraction 0.86), P_aux ≥ 40 MW, and τ_E ≥ 3.5 s whenever T ≤ 10 keV. The heating margin is +5.5 MW. An ignited variant (P_aux ≥ 0, τ_E ≥ 5.5 s) has margin +4.5 MW. The fusion yield is at least 4.8×10⁸ W × t_b.
- **Radial transport comparison principle (T3).** For the flux-surface-averaged heat equation with a no-flux axis and edge Dirichlet data:
  - a stationary strict lower barrier keeps T above it for all time; non-strict and upper-barrier versions also hold;
  - a pair of barriers keeps T inside a two-sided window.
- **Vacuum maximum principle (T1, Milestone A).**
  - The Bochner identity: Δ|B|² = 2|∇B|² for vacuum fields.
  - The weak maximum principle on bounded open sets.
  - A boundary-to-interior bound for field errors. Closed-coil filament fields are instances, given C² regularity as a hypothesis.
- **Building blocks:**
  - Elsässer energy and cross-helicity identities, with conservation under a skew-transport hypothesis.
  - Summation-by-parts (SBP) discrete energy identity, with a checked 4-point operator and a rejected fixture.
  - Available-energy kernel lemmas, and the implication min-B ⇒ max-J for the bounce-action integral expression.
- **Notes corrected and theory notes added** (`notes/theory/`).

None of this is a practical design. M1 is a 0-D model with supplied reactivity data and state-dependent confinement hypotheses. The transport theorems apply to supplied classical solutions, and no barrier has yet been instantiated on a stellarator geometry.

## Corrections and challenged claims

- **Notes** (`Correction (2026-10-20)` markers):
  - Vacuum |B| has no interior *maximum*; interior minima are possible.
  - The flux-surface line bundle is flat.
  - 2-D ideal-MHD regularity is open.
  - The relative-entropy identity in the BKM note is **false** for non-constant T*. An exact sympy counterexample and the corrected identity are in `notes/theory/relative-entropy-dissipation.md`.
  - The available-energy kernel does **not** vanish for opposite-sign frequencies. This is proved in Lean as `availableEnergyKernel_pos_of_opposite_signs`.
- **Review (lane E)** found that the first M1 version ignored fuel dilution while using Z_eff = 1.7, which overstated alpha heating by about 26%. The kernel now has a quasineutral dilution model, and M1 was recomputed (P_aux raised from 20 to 40 MW). Wording was corrected throughout: "for all t ≥ 0" replaces "forever"; "no upper barrier at 30 keV" replaces "in [10,30]"; the satisfiability witness is labelled as vacuous for its τ_E crossing hypotheses, and a new crossing witness actually uses those hypotheses.
- **Holonomy (T10)** verdict: the flux-surface holonomy does not encode ι, so the idea is not useful as stated. Period/Hodge bounds may be useful later (`notes/theory/flux-surface-holonomy.md`).
- **Toroidal min-B obstruction:** sourced to Helander (Rep. Prog. Phys. 77, 087001, 2014): ∇⊥B = Bκ in vacuum, so min-B forces zero field-line curvature (`notes/theory/toroidal-min-b-obstruction.md`).
